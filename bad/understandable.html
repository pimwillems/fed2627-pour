BAD practice — Understandable

Content and controls must be understandable and predictable. This page is neither.

Demo 1

Missing language attribute

This page has no lang attribute on the <html> element. A screen reader has to guess the language — a Dutch screen reader may read this English page with Dutch pronunciation rules, turning it into gibberish.

Demo 2

Meaningless link text

Screen reader users often pull up a list of all links on a page. This is what they get:

  • Our returns policy changed. Click here
  • We published our annual report. Read more
  • Download the price list. Click here
  • New job openings. More

Four links: “Click here, Read more, Click here, More.” Good luck.

Demo 3

Unexpected context change

Merely selecting an option with the arrow keys instantly “navigates”. Keyboard users can never browse the options.


Demo 4

Vague error messages

Leave a field empty and submit. Which field is wrong? What should you fix? The form won’t tell you — and it also wipes what you typed.

ERROR! Invalid input.

Demo 5

Needless jargon

The same message, as some sites write it:

“Per our SLA remediation matrix, asynchronous fulfilment of RMA-adjacent deliverables is contingent upon T+2 settlement of the applicable invoicing artefacts.”

Translation: “We refund you two days after we receive your return.” Plain language is an accessibility feature — for people with cognitive disabilities, non-native speakers, and honestly everyone.
